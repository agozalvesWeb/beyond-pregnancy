Understanding your postpartum body

Birth is followed by a period of physical and emotional recovery. The experience differs after vaginal birth and cesarean birth, and can also vary widely among people with similar birth experiences. Athletes may be eager to return, but healing and function deserve attention before performance goals.

Women's sports medicineEducational guide

Systems that may need attention

  • Pelvic floor: leakage, urgency, heaviness, pain or difficulty coordinating muscles.
  • Abdominal wall: stretching, changes in core function and possible diastasis recti.
  • Musculoskeletal system: changes in strength, mobility, posture and load tolerance.
  • Whole-person recovery: sleep, mood, nutrition, lactation and support.

What recovery can look like

  • Some symptoms improve with time and appropriate support; persistent symptoms deserve assessment.
  • A pelvic health physiotherapist may assess pelvic floor and abdominal function.
  • Cesarean recovery includes healing of abdominal tissues and a surgical incision.

Build a support team

  • Consider your obstetric clinician, sports medicine physician, pelvic health physiotherapist, dietitian and mental health professional as appropriate.
Important: Common symptoms are not automatically harmless. Seek individualized assessment for pain, leakage, pelvic pressure, worsening bleeding or concerns about healing.
