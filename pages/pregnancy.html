Training while pregnant

Pregnancy brings physical and physiological changes that can affect comfort, balance, temperature regulation and exercise tolerance. For many people with uncomplicated pregnancies, staying active can be beneficial. The right activity and intensity depend on medical history, pregnancy course and professional guidance.

Women's sports medicineEducational guide

Understand the changes

  • Cardiovascular and respiratory demands change during pregnancy.
  • Body shape, balance and movement strategies may adapt as pregnancy progresses.
  • Joint discomfort, nausea, fatigue or pelvic symptoms can influence training.

Adapt rather than compete with your previous self

  • Use perceived effort and the talk test rather than chasing pre-pregnancy pace or numbers.
  • Modify position, load, range of motion and impact as needed.
  • Prioritize hydration, temperature management, fueling and recovery.

Know when to stop and seek advice

  • Stop exercise and contact a clinician for vaginal bleeding, fluid leakage, painful contractions, dizziness, chest pain, or unusual shortness of breath.
  • Seek urgent care for severe or alarming symptoms.
Important: Pregnancy and exercise guidance should be individualized. A clinician can advise whether specific activities are appropriate, especially with complications or medical conditions.
