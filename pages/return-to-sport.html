Rebuilding athletic performance

Returning to sport after childbirth is a gradual process. There is no single date that clears every athlete to run, lift, jump or compete. Readiness depends on healing, symptoms, functional capacity, training history, birth experience, sleep, nutrition and mental wellbeing.

Women's sports medicineEducational guide

Build a foundation

  • Start with daily function, comfortable mobility and basic strength when medically appropriate.
  • Assess breathing coordination, trunk control and pelvic floor symptoms.
  • Progress one training variable at a time where possible.

Prepare for impact

  • Running and jumping require tolerance to repeated impact, strength and coordination.
  • Pelvic heaviness, leakage, pain or increased bleeding are reasons to stop or modify and seek assessment.
  • A graded walk-run progression may be appropriate for some athletes after assessment.

Return to sport-specific demands

  • Rebuild acceleration, deceleration, change of direction and sport-specific skills gradually.
  • Monitor symptoms during training and over the following day.
  • Competition readiness includes confidence, recovery capacity and medical guidance.
Important: A checklist cannot medically clear an athlete. Work with qualified professionals to plan an individualized progression.
